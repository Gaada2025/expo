Module 1
Understanding Your Body
What's happening — and why you feel the way you feel

What is Perimenopause?

Perimenopause is the transitional phase leading up to menopause — typically beginning in your early-to-mid 40s, though it can start as early as 35. It's defined by fluctuating and declining estrogen and progesterone levels.

It ends when you've gone 12 consecutive months without a period — that moment is officially called menopause. Everything before that is perimenopause, and it can last 2 to 12 years.

Key Hormones Involved

Estrogen: Regulates mood, sleep, temperature, memory, and bone density.
Progesterone: Calming hormone — when it drops, anxiety and sleep issues rise.
Testosterone: Affects libido, energy, and confidence.
Cortisol: Stress hormone — becomes more reactive during this transition.

Common Symptoms

Physical

  • Irregular periods
  • Hot flashes / night sweats
  • Sleep disruption
  • Weight changes
  • Joint aches
  • Hair thinning
  • Skin changes

Cognitive & Emotional

  • Brain fog
  • Anxiety / low mood
  • Irritability
  • Memory lapses
  • Low motivation
  • Loss of confidence
  • Feeling unlike yourself

Module 3 · Sleep Protocol
The Sleep Reset
Reclaiming your rest
Why sleep suffers in perimenopause

Declining progesterone (your calming hormone) and fluctuating estrogen directly disrupt sleep architecture. You spend less time in deep, restorative sleep — leading to that exhausted-but-wired feeling.

Sleep Hygiene Checklist

  • Room temperature 16–19°C (cool is better)
  • No screens 60 minutes before bed
  • Blackout curtains or eye mask
  • No caffeine after 1pm
  • Consistent wake time (even weekends)
  • Magnesium glycinate supplement (consult doctor)
  • Cooling mattress pad or fan
  • Herbal tea: chamomile, valerian, or passionflower

Module 5 · Doctor Prep
Your Appointment Toolkit
Walk in prepared. Walk out with answers.
Before your appointment

Bring this page (or your completed tracker pages) to show your doctor the patterns you've observed. Specific data leads to better diagnoses and treatment options.

Pre-Appointment Checklist

  • Bring 4+ weeks of completed tracker pages
  • Write down your top 5 most disruptive symptoms
  • Note when symptoms started and any patterns
  • List all current medications and supplements
  • Write down your questions in advance (see below)
  • Bring someone with you if you want support

Questions to Ask Your Doctor

  • "Based on my symptoms and age, could this be perimenopause?"
  • "What hormone tests do you recommend and what will they tell us?"
  • "What are my options — HRT, non-hormonal, lifestyle?"
  • "What are the risks and benefits of HRT for someone like me?"
  • "How long might this transition last?"
  • "What should I monitor and when should I come back?"
